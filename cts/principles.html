Principles

A few rules CTS follows. Most of them came from a concrete mistake or a concrete risk.

Human in the loop

The machine prepares, a person decides. CTS reads thousands of postings, scores them, builds a CV, drafts answers and fills in the form. It never sends anything on its own:

  • I pick which openings to apply to.
  • The CV is assembled from blocks I have proofread, by rules agreed in advance.
  • I review the answers on the form.
  • Nothing is sent without my explicit go-ahead for that specific application. I review the filled forms, often in one sitting, and say what to change before it goes.
  • Emails, messages and connection requests are drafts until I send them.

This is slower than a bot that applies everywhere, and that is the point. A company receives an application that a person chose and checked.

No facts I did not approve

A CV is assembled from a library of blocks I wrote or approved. An adapted CV may change wording to match a posting, but it may not add a skill, a number or a year of experience that is not in the library. The code checks this, not only the prompt: a term without evidence is rejected.

My own words means my own words

Application forms often ask for an answer in your own words: why this company, a project you are proud of, how you would approach something. When a form asks for that, I write the answer myself, and no LLM is involved at all. Not to draft it, not to polish it. If a company wants to hear me, it gets me.

No LinkedIn automation

No agent of mine opens LinkedIn, scrolls it or reads it on my behalf. LinkedIn's terms forbid automation, and my account is worth more than any feed. CTS uses only what LinkedIn sends me by email, and pages I save myself while browsing.

My data stays on my computer

CTS runs locally. Email access is read-only. Screenshots on this site are real, with company names, people and email content pixelated.

Rules change through cases

When a score looks wrong, I record the case, find the cause and change the rule, and the change is dated. The scoring never depends on my mood that day.
