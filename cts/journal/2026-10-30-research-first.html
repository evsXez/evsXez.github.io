Research first, then a hub

30 October 2026 · written on 13 November 2026, looking back

I left my job at the end of October and started the search on 28 October. Instead of opening job boards, I spent the first three days on research, with AI agents doing the reading in parallel: what roles exist for someone like me, how applicant tracking systems filter people, where openings are actually published, which companies in Barcelona fit, how the market changed since 2022.

Two findings shaped everything after:

  • Openings live on company career pages first. Most of those pages run on a handful of applicant tracking systems with public APIs, so they can be read directly and cheaply.
  • Automatic rejection is rarer than people think. What filters you out is the knockout questions on the form (location, work permit, salary) and then a human skimming for thirty seconds.

On 30 October I looked closely at career-ops, the best-known open-source job search system. It is impressive and huge, and built for the opposite approach: a long report on each of a few openings. I wanted a cheap score for thousands of openings and a fast queue. So I decided to write my own and borrow parts of theirs.

The same day a small local hub appeared: a calendar of the plan with checkboxes and a "today" panel. That hub became CTS.
