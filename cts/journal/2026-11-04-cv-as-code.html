The CV is a build artifact

4 November 2026 · written on 13 November 2026, looking back

I stopped editing a CV document. Since 3 November the CV is built from a library of blocks: summary variants, bullets per job, skills, titles. A script picks the blocks, a linter checks the result (length, dates, banned words, repeated verbs) and headless Chrome renders a text-based PDF. One click on a job card builds a CV for that job.

Then I fed the PDF to the parsers that companies actually use, without sending anything. Two surprises:

  • Chrome joined "fi" and "fl" into ligatures. Humans read them fine; a parser broke "Remote Config" around the ligature and read "and fixed" as "andfixed". Turning ligatures off in the PDF step fixed it.
  • An online AI detector rated the new experience section 68% AI. My CV from 2023, written by hand before I used LLMs, got 70%. I stopped worrying about detectors and kept the rule that matters: no fact in the CV that I did not approve.
